# Data Privacy & Confidentiality Reflection

## Research & Learn

**Key takeaways from Focus Bear's privacy policy**

Focus Bear is based in Australia but structures its privacy practices around GDPR, since it has EU users, and has appointed an external company (by Data) as both its EU representative and Data Protection Officer. The policy breaks down exactly what categories of personal data get collected, things like identification data (email, login), lifestyle/habit data, occupation, marketing preferences, technical/device data, payment info, and even a special category covering health and religious inferences (for example, a habit like "take insulin" or "read Quran" could imply health or religious information, even though it's not asked for directly).

What stood out most is how specific the safeguards are per data type rather than one blanket policy: habit data is double encrypted, payment details aren't stored by Focus Bear directly at all (handled entirely by Stripe), survey responses are anonymised, and crash logs are stripped of personal information automatically. It's a good example of matching the level of protection to the sensitivity of the data rather than treating everything the same way.

**What types of data are considered confidential at Focus Bear**

Based on the policy, this includes user identification data, lifestyle and habit data, occupation and survey responses, financial/payment data, device and technical logs, and the special category data covering health conditions or religious belief that can be inferred from habits or optional surveys (like the ADHD survey mentioned in the policy). Internally, this would also extend to company specific information I come across as an intern, credentials, internal documentation, and anything not meant for public or external tools.

**Best practices for handling confidential data**

Only collect and access what's actually necessary for the task at hand, store it securely rather than in plain text or personal notes, don't share it outside of approved tools or people, and dispose of it properly once it's no longer needed rather than letting it sit around. The policy also reflects this principle directly: data is only kept as long as needed to fulfill the purpose it was collected for, not indefinitely by default.

**How to respond to a suspected data breach or accidental disclosure**

Report it immediately rather than trying to quietly fix it alone, since the longer sensitive data is exposed the bigger the risk. Focus Bear's own policy describes a formal breach management process, evaluating, managing, and reporting potential security breaches to the relevant supervisory authority under GDPR, which shows this isn't treated as optional or informal internally. As an intern, that means telling my manager or the relevant contact straight away if I suspect something was exposed or shared incorrectly, rather than assuming it will resolve itself.

## Reflection

**What steps can I take to ensure I handle data securely in my daily tasks?**

Being deliberate about what I paste into AI tools or chat windows is the most relevant one for me right now. It's easy to copy an error message or a snippet of real data into an AI assistant for a quick answer without thinking about whether that data is sensitive, company specific, or user related. Going forward, the habit is to strip out or generalise anything identifying before pasting it anywhere external, and to treat "would I be comfortable with this being fully public" as the quick test before sharing.

**How should I store, share, and dispose of sensitive information safely?**

Store it only where it's meant to be, approved company tools and systems, not personal notes, personal cloud storage, or random files on my own machine. Share it only with people who actually need it for the task, not broadly by default. Dispose of it properly once it's no longer needed rather than leaving copies scattered across old files or messages, similar to how Focus Bear's own policy ties data retention to how long the data is actually needed for its stated purpose.

**What are some common mistakes that lead to data privacy issues, and how can they be avoided?**

Pasting real data into external tools without thinking it through, using overly broad access or sharing permissions instead of the minimum needed, leaving devices unlocked, and holding onto data longer than necessary "just in case." Most of these come down to convenience winning over caution in the moment. The fix isn't really about knowing the rules, it's building the pause, checking before sharing or pasting something rather than after.

## Task

**Habit adopted:** being careful about what I paste into AI tools or chats, checking first whether it contains anything sensitive, company specific, or user related, and generalising or removing that detail before sharing it externally.

**Key learning I will implement:** treat data protection as proportional to sensitivity, the way Focus Bear's own policy does (habit data double encrypted, payment data not stored at all, crash logs stripped of personal info) rather than applying one flat level of caution to everything. In practice that means being most careful specifically with anything that could identify a person or reveal something about them, even indirectly.
